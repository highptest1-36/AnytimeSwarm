\begin{abstract}
Multi-UAV replanners usually plan for nominal execution. Under wind, speed and service-time errors, such plans promise
finish times that the vehicles do not achieve, and tasks near the end of a route are served late. This paper proposes
AnytimeSwarm-CC, a replanner that plans against a chance constraint on the finish time of every task. Because the mean
and the spread of a calibrated finish-time error grow linearly with the lead time, the chance constraint reduces exactly
to an effective deadline per task. The margin therefore grows along a route, and the fast repair and adaptive large
neighbourhood search of the nominal model are reused unchanged. A nominal fallback keeps the tasks that the constraint
would give up. The replanner was evaluated in pre-registered studies on held-out missions of a kinematic simulator with
disturbances and communication latency. In \val{KNRuns} runs, the chance-constrained model served \val{KDabsBuf} to
\val{KDabsForty} percentage points more reward than each of six baselines, among them metareasoning stopping rules and a
constant time buffer (Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$). Ablations attributed the gain to the planning model rather than to the
stopping rule. On new seeds, the fallback added \val{LDabsPropAll} percentage points and served \val{LURelConv}\% more
reward than a conventional event-triggered planner, at the price of more late tasks. In PX4 software-in-the-loop flights
with Gazebo and ROS~2, the replanner served \val{KCLateRedForty}\% fewer tasks late, but the utility differences on
\val{KCN} missions stayed within the noise.
\end{abstract}
