\begin{abstract}
Multi-UAV replanners usually plan for nominal execution and stop their search after a fixed time budget. Under wind,
speed and service-time errors, such plans promise more than the vehicles deliver. This paper proposes AnytimeSwarm-CC, a
replanner that replans on events and periodically, plans against a chance constraint on the finish time of every task,
and stops an anytime adaptive large neighbourhood search with a learned rule. Because the mean and the spread of a
calibrated finish-time error grow linearly with the lead time, the chance constraint reduces exactly to an effective
deadline per task, so fast heuristics are reused unchanged. Three pre-registered studies on held-out seeds use a
kinematic simulator and PX4 software-in-the-loop with Gazebo and ROS~2. In
\val{KNRuns} kinematic runs, the proposed replanner served \val{KDabsBuf} to \val{KDabsForty} percentage points more
reward than six baselines, among them metareasoning stopping rules and a robust time buffer (Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$),
and \val{KURelConv}\% more than a conventional planner. Ablations attribute the gain to the chance-constrained model,
on which the learned stopping rule was equivalent to a fixed budget. In flights of \val{KCN} missions, the proposed
replanner served \val{KCLateRedForty}\% fewer tasks late, but utility differences stayed within the noise. Under high
disturbances with halved time windows, the model was too cautious. On new seeds, a nominal fallback for the tasks that the
chance constraint gives up served \val{LDabsPropAll} percentage points more reward than the replanner without it and \val{LDabsBufHighWHalf} more than the buffer in that regime (Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$), at the
price of more late tasks.
\end{abstract}
