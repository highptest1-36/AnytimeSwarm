\begin{abstract}
Multi-UAV replanners usually plan for nominal execution and stop their search after a fixed time budget. Under wind,
speed and service-time errors, such plans promise more than the vehicles deliver. This paper proposes AnytimeSwarm-CC, a
replanner that takes three decisions in every replanning cycle. It replans on events and periodically, plans against a
chance constraint on the finish time of every task, and stops an anytime adaptive large neighbourhood search when a
learned prediction of the next improvement no longer exceeds a delay cost. Because the mean and the spread of a
calibrated finish-time error grow linearly with the lead time, the chance constraint reduces exactly to an effective
deadline per task, and the fast repair and search heuristics are reused unchanged. The evaluation uses a kinematic
simulator with disturbances drawn with common random numbers and PX4 software-in-the-loop with Gazebo and ROS~2, and two
pre-registered studies with frozen code and held-out seeds. In \val{KNRuns} kinematic runs, the proposed replanner
reached a utility of \val{KUProp}\%. It served \val{KDabsBuf} to \val{KDabsForty} percentage points more reward than
six baselines, among them stopping rules from the metareasoning literature and a robust time buffer (all Holm-adjusted
$p<0.001$), \val{KURelConv}\% more than a conventional planner, and \val{KLateRedForty}\% fewer tasks late than a fixed budget of
40\,ms. Ablations attribute the gain to the chance-constrained model. On this model, the learned stopping rule was
equivalent to a fixed budget of 40\,ms, and under high disturbances with halved time windows the model was too cautious
and a constant buffer served more reward. \val{KAbstractTierC}
\end{abstract}
