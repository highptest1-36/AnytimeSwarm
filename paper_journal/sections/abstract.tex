\begin{abstract}
Multi-UAV replanners usually stop their search after a fixed time budget. This paper studies when such a planner should
stop computing and publish its plan, with exact and with uncertain execution. AnytimeSwarm repairs the previous plan,
improves it with an anytime adaptive large neighbourhood search and stops when a learned prediction of the next
improvement no longer exceeds a delay cost derived from the slack of the plan. AnytimeSwarm-R adds a calibrated model of
finish-time errors and dissemination latency to this cost. The evaluation has two tiers, a kinematic simulator with
wind, speed, service and latency disturbances drawn with common random numbers, and PX4 software-in-the-loop with
Gazebo and ROS~2 after a rescaling that preserves every dimensionless ratio. All design choices were frozen before the
held-out runs. With exact execution, AnytimeSwarm reached a regret of \val{EqRegretAS}\% against \val{EqRegretBaseMin}\%
to \val{EqRegretBaseMax}\% for fixed budgets and stagnation stopping at equal computation. In \val{JNRunsMain} kinematic
runs with uncertain execution, search raised the utility from \val{JURepair}\% with plan repair alone to
\val{JURisk}\% with AnytimeSwarm-R, and AnytimeSwarm-R lost \val{JLostRedFixed}\% fewer tasks to late plans than a 250\,ms budget on a slow planner. It
reached the highest mean utility with \val{JMsRedAS}\% less computation than AnytimeSwarm, but its advantage over the
strongest baselines, at most \val{JDmFixedFortyAbs} percentage points, was not significant after Holm correction, and it was not
confirmed with 40 UAVs or in \val{JNCRuns} PX4 flights. Periodic replanning raised the utility of every method by
\val{JTrigMinAbs} to \val{JTrigMaxAbs} points. The kinematic tier predicted the utility of PX4 flights closely ($r=$
\val{JBCr}) but not differences below one point. Adaptive stopping thus mainly saves computation, and the replanning
trigger matters more for the reward served.
\end{abstract}
